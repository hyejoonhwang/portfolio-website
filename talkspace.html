Talkspace Provider Reviews

Redesigning the provider card so members can choose a provider with confidence.

As Talkspace's product design intern, I designed how client reviews help members choose a provider. A link to a full reviews page is in development now. After the handoff, I designed and tested a card that works with 15 reviews or none.

Role

Product Design Intern

Timeline

Summer 2026

Team

Me, my design manager, 1 PM, 2 engineers

Tools

Claude Code, FigJam

Problem

40%of auto-matched members never reach a first session, even with the best matching method.

Solution

Let members choose their provider, and show what clients say on the card they choose from.

Before

Intake→ Auto-match→ Book a first session→ First session

With this project

Intake→ Choose your provider→ Book a first session→ First session

01 · The bet

I bet on reviews, because members who pick their own provider show up more

Members who switched to a provider they picked completed a first session at 73–76%. The card lists the same facts for every provider, and none of it says what working with them is like. Client reviews do.

Switchers are more proactive, so it isn't one-to-one, but the gap pointed to choice.

First-session completion, range across matching methods. Source: Talkspace data team, July 2026.

02 · Three directions

I tested three ways to show what clients wrote, never a star score

My PRD's main rule: clients' words, never a rating, since a visible 4.4 can unfairly hurt a good provider. I tested three directions with 18 participants across 3 rounds. All 18 said the reviews strongly influenced their choice.

03 · The constraint

Then our PM flagged that 92% of providers had too few reviews to fill the card

65% had just one review, and many were blank or as short as "Aaron IS GREAT." A review-led card would look empty for most providers and penalize new ones. It had to work the same with 15 reviews or none.

8%3 or more meaningful reviews
92%Fewer than 3

1,461 providers · 5-star review export

04 · The MVP

Our PM and I chose a link to a full reviews page and kept reviews off the card

A "Client Testimonials" link gives every provider the same experience. It was the lightest version that could ship and still shows members reviews. The cost: one more tap.

In the design review I led, PMs, engineers and data researchers renamed the "Read 15 reviews" link and shaped the no-reviews state.

05 · After the handoff

After the handoff, I designed a card for providers with 15 reviews or none

With fewer than 3 relevant reviews, a summary would just reword one or two people, so the card shows the provider's own words.

This answer led me to summarize by intake concern:

"If they were able to help someone else with the same issues, hopefully they could help me."

Test participant

I tested it with 9 people in October. Early signs it helps new providers, though 5 of 9 ruled out men, Sam included.

4 of 9would book Sam, who has no reviews, because of what he wrote.

7 of 9could tell Sam's block was in his own words.

6 of 9felt surer with times shown on the card, as the current design does.

Where it stands

The reviews link is in development, and the card redesign has had one test

No data yet; Talkspace will track first sessions and three sessions in 30 days.

What I learned

To make room for the green block, I moved open times behind a button. Most testers missed them, so the next version keeps both on the card.
